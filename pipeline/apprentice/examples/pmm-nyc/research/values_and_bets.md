# Agentio: stated values and the bets its leadership is making

Note: the Series B (Nov 2025) was $40M led by Forerunner; $56M is total raised, at a $340M valuation [2][9]. Founders: Arthur Leopold (CEO, ex-Cameo), Jonathan Meyers (CTO, ex-Spotify) [3][10]. The Ashby board did not render; its "About" text came from a mirrored posting [6].

## Mission and values in their words

- Careers page headline: "Build something generational"; "An $800B market. A once-in-a-generation technology shift. This is the moment." [1]
- Six named values on the careers page: "Build the Future, Move at Unprecedented Speed"; "Obsess Over Impact"; "Run Through Walls"; "Always Be Lore Maxxing"; "Take Intelligent Risks, Learn Relentlessly"; "No Ego, All In — for Agentio" [1].
- In person from NoMad, NYC: "We believe the best work, just like the best advertising, is human." [1]
- Job-board boilerplate: "Agentio enables marketers to buy creator led ads as easily and scalably as Meta or Google ads" and aims to "transform a $800B digital ad ecosystem by shifting digital ad spend into the hands of creators" [6]. Creator-side mission: creators should "live off their work" and be "paid in proportion to the value they create" [6]. "At Agentio, data drives decisions" [6].
- Series B letter: the team is "world-class, customer-obsessed, insatiably curious, and wildly ambitious" [2].
- Leopold: "Focus on authenticity by creating ads that feel genuine"; "Prioritize performance, rather than reach" [12].

## What investors said

- Forerunner (Eurie Kim, lead): "Agentio is building the infrastructure needed for creator-led ad development and monetization at scale," likened to Google and Meta unlocking search and social [2]; "We'd been tracking Agentio for over a year" [9]; multi-channel support was "a clear signal" it could be an AI media-planning platform [9]. Forerunner's page cites a "structurally inefficient" market, network effects ("every new brand attracts higher-quality creators") and revenue "tied to ad spend rather than fixed fees" [7].
- Benchmark (Sarah Tavel, Series A lead): "The creator economy is one of those spaces that has eluded a true marketplace"; past founders "ended up in the quicksand of being an agency" [8].
- AlleyCorp (seed co-lead with Craft): "the first ad platform for creator content that is operating at scale"; likens it to DoubleClick for display [10]. No Craft write-up was found.
- Forbes Next Billion-Dollar Startups 2025: $16M raised, estimated 2024 revenue $2M, lead investors AlleyCorp, Benchmark, Craft [11].

## The bets

**Bet 1: creator ads can be bought and measured with performance-channel rigor.** Leopold: "the future of advertising is creators telling a brand's story, not more banner, display, and skippable video ads" [12]; brands can scale "from $50K/month, to $1M/month or more" without losing efficiency [12].
- Evidence so far: company-reported case studies (Bombas 5.3x ROAS vs digital video; Turo and Suri cite MMM lift) [5][2]; tracking via links, QR codes and Shopify conversions [8]; profitability claimed at Series A [8][16]; 100+ enterprise brands, 5x YoY growth in 2025 [2][15].
- Not yet shown: any independently verified ROAS or incrementality study; revenue since Series A (Forbes estimated $2M for 2024) [11][16]; pricing rests on creators' median views, which Leopold called "very much a finger-in-the-wind situation" in 2024 [16].

**Bet 2: AI content understanding can price and allocate creator inventory.** Meyers: "You only are able to hit that scale if you're able to automate each phase of the lifecycle" [13]; "AI can now automate the manual administrative tasks that once took weeks or months" [15]; the company says it trains "LLM-driven ranking and pricing models" on first-party signals [6] and is "integrating reasoning models and multimodal understanding" [9].
- Evidence so far: an AI review agent checks drafts against brief and brand-safety rules; time to first bid cut from 45-50 days to under a day; 98% of deliverables approved on time (company figures) [9][16].
- Not yet shown: model accuracy, pricing error, or a benchmark against human buyers; the "hundreds of thousands of pieces of content" claim was not found in any read source; FTC-disclosure liability for automated approvals is untested [16].

**Bet 3: brands will shift budget to creators at scale, and a marketplace will compound.** Series B letter: creators get under 2% of digital ad spend (~$10B of ~$800B) and creator-led ads "will move hundreds of billions of dollars over the next decade"; cites Unilever's CEO planning 50% of marketing to creators [2][6]. Network-effects claim: more brands, more creators, better models [2][7].
- Evidence so far: Unilever's Fernando Fernandez announced (March 2025) moving social from ~30% to ~50% of budget with 20x more influencers [17]; "tens of millions" moved by 100+ brands in 2025 [9]; Meta Partnership Ads in beta, TikTok and Snap planned for 2026 [9].
- Not yet shown: Unilever's 50% covers paid social as a whole, not creators alone, and Gartner doubted others will follow at that scale [17]; no Unilever-scale advertiser on Agentio; TikTok and Snap not live as of September 2026 [16]; sell-side competition for slots asserted by Meyers but not quantified [8].

## Risks the press names

- Platform dependence: pricing and APIs rely on YouTube and Meta policies, e.g. YouTube's August 2026 change to view counting [16].
- Agency quicksand (Tavel); Publicis (Influential, Captiv8) bundling creator media; GRIN and CreatorIQ adding self-serve [8][16].
- Large creators bypassing the 20% take to deal direct; a budget pullback would show first as fewer bids [16].
- Most metrics are company-reported and unverified [9][14].
